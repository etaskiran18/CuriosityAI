# Doubt, Belief, and the Motion of Inquiry

One way to understand inquiry is as movement from unsettled doubt toward more stable belief. A question becomes powerful when it makes the system aware of an instability in its current view. This instability can be logical, empirical, ethical, or conceptual.

In a closed-loop architecture, doubt can be operationalized as a detected mismatch: disagreement between sources, contradiction between earlier memory and new evidence, or failure of a synthesis to explain its own assumptions. Curiosity then becomes the mechanism that selects which mismatch is worth investigating.

However, not every doubt is equally valuable. Some doubts are too broad, too vague, or impossible to research. The planning agent should prefer doubts that can be transformed into focused next topics. The evaluator should penalize questions that do not lead to further inquiry.
