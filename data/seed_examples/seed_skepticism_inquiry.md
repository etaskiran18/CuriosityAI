# Skepticism as a Constraint on Curiosity

Skepticism is not only the refusal to believe; it can also be a method for improving inquiry. A skeptical agent asks whether a question is precise, whether an answer is justified, and whether a claim depends on hidden assumptions. Without skepticism, curiosity may produce many questions but no intellectual progress.

The productive form of skepticism does not destroy inquiry. It filters it. It separates questions that merely sound profound from questions that expose a genuine conceptual problem. In a multi-agent curiosity system, the critic agent plays this role: it prevents the loop from confusing novelty with depth.

There is a tension between skeptical restraint and exploratory freedom. Too much restraint can stop inquiry before new ideas emerge. Too much freedom can produce incoherent wandering. A good loop controller must therefore balance exploration and exploitation: continue promising tensions while occasionally branching into new conceptual territory.
